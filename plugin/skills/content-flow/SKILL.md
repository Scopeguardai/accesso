# Content flow

Plan what the deck says before deciding how it looks.

## 1. Understand the brief

Work out, in one sentence each: who the audience is, what they should think or do
afterwards, and what they already know. If the brief leaves one of these open, choose the
most reasonable reading and record it as a content suggestion at the end.

## 2. Gather material

Search the knowledge base before writing anything. Run several searches with different
words: the topic, the audience's concern, and each point the brief says must be included.
Read the entries that look relevant in full.

- Use only facts that appear in the brief or the knowledge base.
- The brief wins when it disagrees with the knowledge base. Record the conflict as a
  suggestion.
- Treat entries marked `draft` as unconfirmed: you may use them, and you must raise a
  suggestion asking for them to be checked.
- When material for a point is missing, do not fill the gap with plausible detail. Keep
  the slide general and raise a suggestion naming exactly what is missing.

## 3. Shape the story

Pick the arc that fits the goal:

- **Persuade** (pitch, proposal): situation, tension, answer, proof, ask.
- **Inform** (update, report): headline first, then the evidence behind it, then what
  happens next.
- **Introduce** (company or team overview): who we are, what we do, why it matters, how
  to reach us.

Each slide carries one idea, and its headline states that idea as a claim, not a topic:
"Every site adds to one infrastructure base", not "Our sites".

## 4. Write the outline

Record the outline before designing: one entry per slide with a working title, the one
thing the audience must take from it, and the layout that fits. Match the slide count in
the brief. Start with the cover and end with the closing slide; both count.
